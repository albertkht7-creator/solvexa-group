# Sales Rep Job Interview: A Step-by-Step Guide

A job interview for a sales rep is a sales situation: the candidate is selling themselves. That is why it is so easy to be taken in by a good first impression. Below is an interview plan that helps you assess **facts and behaviour**, not likeability. It is designed for about an hour.

Before you start, have your candidate profile ready. I explain how to build one in [the B2B salesperson profile](/en/blog/b2b-salesperson-profile). Without a profile, you have nothing to compare the answers against.

## Before the interview

- Read the CV with an eye for numbers: targets, attainment, size of clients, time spent in each role.
- Mark 3-4 points you want to probe (gaps, frequent job changes, vague wording).
- Choose 6-8 questions from the list of [salesperson interview questions](/en/blog/salesperson-interview-questions).
- Prepare a simulation scenario (I describe it in [sales simulation in recruitment](/en/blog/sales-simulation-in-recruitment)).
- Prepare a scorecard with the criteria from the profile.

## Interview plan

**1. Opening (about 5 minutes).** Introduce the company, the role and how the meeting will run. Give the candidate a moment to relax. Notice whether they open with their own confidence or with an effort to understand what you are looking for.

**2. Experience and numbers (about 15 minutes).** Go through the last two or three roles. Ask about targets, attainment, conversion and sales cycle. Probe if an answer is general. A good salesperson answers specifically and without hesitation.

**3. A lost deal and the lessons (about 10 minutes).** Ask for the story of a deal the candidate lost but could have won. Watch whether they take responsibility and what they changed afterwards. This is one of the best questions in the whole interview.

**4. Simulation (about 15 minutes).** Play the client and let the candidate sell. This is the moment when you see the difference between someone who talks about selling and someone who sells. I explain why a simulation matters more than the interview alone in [Recruiting a salesperson: how to spot a good one](/en/blog/how-to-spot-a-good-salesperson).

**5. Candidate's questions (about 5-10 minutes).** Pay attention to what they ask. A candidate who is good at sales asks about clients, process and goals, not only about pay and benefits.

## What to watch for during the interview

- **Do they know their numbers**, or do they only tell stories?
- **Do they take responsibility**, or blame the circumstances?
- **Do they listen**, or just wait for their turn to speak?
- **Do they push for a concrete next step**, for example by asking what the rest of the process looks like?
- **Are they self-driven**, meaning they develop on their own instead of waiting for training?

## The most common recruiter mistakes

- **The interview turns into a chat.** A pleasant atmosphere is not an assessment criterion.
- **You talk too much.** If the recruiter talks more than the candidate, you learn nothing.
- **No simulation.** You decide on the basis of stories.
- **Judging on the spot, from impressions.** Write your notes straight after the interview, before the emotions fade.
- **Different questions for different candidates.** Then you cannot compare them.

## After the interview: assessment within 24 hours

Fill in the scorecard immediately after the meeting. For each essential trait, write down a score and **one specific example** you base it on. If you cannot give an example, you cannot give a score.

If more than one person is assessing, have them complete their scorecards independently before they talk. Only then compare conclusions.
