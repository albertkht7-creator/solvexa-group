# The Demo You Run Too Early Costs You the Deal

There is a moment in a meeting when a salesperson feels relief: "finally, I can show the product". And very often that is exactly the moment they lose the deal - because they are showing it two minutes too early.

The demo is the most powerful tool you have. And that is precisely why you must not waste it by putting it on the table before the customer even knows why they need it.

## The demo is the reward, not the opening

A customer who hasn't yet told you what they need will watch your demo like an advert - politely, indifferently, without engagement. They have already seen ten like it. They all looked the same.

The same customer, having spent the first part of the meeting telling you about their problem, will watch exactly the same demo in a completely different way. Because now they are not watching a product - **they are watching the solution to their problem**. It is not a question of what you show. It is a question of when.

## Don't show everything. Show that one thing

The second mistake is a demo that shows everything. Every feature, every tab, every possibility. The salesperson is proud of how much the product can do. The customer is overwhelmed and will remember nothing.

A good demo is brutally selective. You show the two or three things that answer what the customer told you earlier - and you skip the rest, however impressive it is. Less is more, because the customer doesn't buy the number of features. They buy the feeling that their specific problem has just disappeared.

That is why a demo without prior discovery is impossible to do well - you don't know what to cut, so you show everything.

## The order that sells

First understand. Then show - but only what fits. It sounds trivial, until you count how many salespeople do the opposite: they show everything before they have understood anything.

I won't break down here how to build a demo for a specific customer - we do that in practice, on your product and your calls. But before you next reach for the screen, ask yourself one question: **does the customer already know why they should want to see this?** If not - it is too early.
