# What I Learned Listening to Hundreds of My Salespeople's Calls

One of my standing habits - both for myself and in the teams I have led - is listening back to sales calls and taking them apart together, piece by piece. Not to judge anyone. But to see what separates a call that ends in a sale from one that ends in "just send me the offer by email".

After hundreds of these sessions, patterns emerge. And they are different from what most people assume.

## Results don't depend on who talks better

The most talkative salesperson is rarely the best. That is the first thing that strikes you when you start listening to calls instead of looking at CVs.

The salespeople who deliver are not the loudest or the most brilliant. They are the most **attentive**. They ask a question and actually listen to the answer, instead of waiting for their turn. They catch the sentence the customer tossed out in passing in the third minute, and come back to it in the twentieth. It is that attentiveness, not eloquence, that makes the difference.

## Most deals die not on price, but on structure

The second thing: most lost calls are not lost on price or on objections. They are lost on a **lack of structure**.

A conversation without structure falls apart. The salesperson jumps from product to price, goes back to the product, loses the thread, the customer loses their bearings and - naturally - postpones the decision. A conversation with a clear skeleton leads the customer by the hand from problem to solution. Same salesperson, same product, same customer - a different result, because of a different structure.

This, by the way, is the best news for anyone who thinks they "don't have a knack for sales". Structure can be learned. The knack is overrated.

## Feedback has to be honest, or it does harm

The third point is about building a team. A culture in which nobody tells anybody they did something wrong looks nice - and is toxic. If we praise a call that was weak, we are not only doing harm to that one person. We are doing it to everyone who learns from it and takes the mistake for a model.

That is why, in the teams I have led, feedback was direct - diplomatic, but honest. Not to bring anyone down, but because a salesperson who doesn't hear the truth about their call will repeat the same mistake a hundred times.

## Why this matters to you

If you manage a sales team and have the feeling that results are a lottery - it isn't a lottery. It is the lack of a system for listening to, breaking down and improving calls. Results don't come from motivation at the morning call. They come from someone sitting down with a salesperson over their real call and showing them exactly what to change.

I won't lay out here the whole process I use when working with teams - that is precisely the work I do in my consulting and training. But if you remember just one thing from this: **stop judging your salespeople by how they speak in the team meeting. Start listening to how they talk to the customer.** That is where the whole truth about your sales is.
