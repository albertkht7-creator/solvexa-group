# You Lose the Sale in the First Two Minutes - and You Don't Even Notice

I have run thousands of sales conversations and I know one thing: **the first two minutes decide the outcome of the meeting** - not the moment you name your price.

Most salespeople open the same way: they introduce themselves, introduce the company, move on to the product. It sounds professional. And it's a mistake, because at that point the customer doesn't yet know why they should listen to you - and you're already talking.

## Don't start with the product. Start with the agenda

Before I say anything about what I sell, I do one thing that 90% of salespeople skip: **I set the agenda**.

I tell the customer plainly how the meeting will go. That first I want to understand their company and what they need. That then I'll show what I can realistically offer. And that we'll get to price at the end - because only then will it make sense.

It's a simple move, and it does three things at once:

- **You take control** of the conversation without taking it away from the customer.
- **You remove the tension** - the customer knows they're not about to be hit with a price.
- **You build credibility** - someone with a plan looks like an expert, not a pushy salesman.

## Then listen - properly, not for show

After setting the agenda I don't move on to the pitch. I hand the floor to the customer and ask about their business: how it works, what hurts, what they need. And I stop talking.

This is the part you can't fake. A weak salesperson asks a question and waits for their turn to get back to the presentation. A good salesperson **genuinely listens**, because those answers are what they'll build the whole sale on later. Everything you say in the second half of the meeting should rest on what you heard in the first.

A customer who spent the first minutes talking about their problems is in a completely different frame of mind towards the solution than one to whom you've been reciting your offer from the moment they walked in.

## Why this works

Because selling isn't persuading. It's matching. And to match something, you first need to know what you're matching it to.

A salesperson who starts with the product sells everyone the same thing. A salesperson who starts with the customer sells each one exactly what they need - with the same products, but told in a completely different way.

I won't lay out here the whole structure of a first meeting or the specific questions I use - we work through that in training, on your real conversations. But even this one change - **agenda and discovery instead of a pitch at the door** - can turn around the meetings you're losing today without knowing why.

Start with the customer. The product can wait.
