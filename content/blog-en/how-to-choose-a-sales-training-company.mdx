# How to Choose a Sales Training Company: 6 Questions to Ask Before You Sign

The short answer: **you can recognise a good sales training company by the fact that its trainer has sold personally - in the live market, not on slides - and takes responsibility for the result, not just for "delivering the workshop".** If you only ask one question, ask how much this person has personally sold.

Below are six questions that, in a few minutes, separate real training from well-directed theatre. Ask them before you sign anything.

## 1. Has the trainer actually sold - and what exactly?

This is the first and most important question. Most sales trainers have never worked in sales - the only thing they have sold is their own training. They teach techniques from books that they have never tested under target pressure.

Ask for specifics: what did they sell, to whom, for how long, with what result. A practitioner will answer without hesitation. A theorist will start talking about "methodology".

## 2. Will I get implementation, or just a presentation?

A nice workshop after which the team goes back to their desks and does everything the old way is a waste of money. Ask directly: what happens **after** the training? Is there work on your real calls, on your live pipeline, with specific corrections?

Real training leaves a change in behaviour, not a file of slides. If the offer ends at a "workshop day", you are buying an event, not a result.

## 3. Will the training be tailored to my company?

The same programme for a software house, a wholesaler and a manufacturer is a warning sign. Selling looks different in each of them.

A good trainer will first get to know your business, your process and where you actually lose deals - and only then propose a programme. If you hear a ready-made package before anyone has asked how you sell, this is not training for you. It is a template.

## 4. Who exactly will deliver the training?

It happens that a well-known name sells you the training while someone else runs the workshop - a junior trainer reading from the same script. Ask directly who will stand in front of your team and what experience they have.

You want someone who has sat in the trenches of sales, not someone who finished a trainer course yesterday.

## 5. How will we measure whether it worked?

If nobody talks about measuring the effect, it means nobody plans to take responsibility for it. Agree up front how you will know the training worked: more meetings booked, higher conversion, a shorter sales cycle, fewer deals lost on price.

The point is not to measure everything to the last percentage. The point is to have a conversation about results at all, rather than just about "participant satisfaction".

## 6. Will the trainer tell me straight that it's not a fit?

It sounds paradoxical, but the best sign that you have found the right person is their readiness to say, "In your case, training won't help right now - you need to fix X first." Someone who sells you training for every situation is selling for themselves, not for you.

A practitioner who takes responsibility for results would rather turn down an assignment than take one that will not deliver.
