# 5 Cold Calling Mistakes That Kill Your Results

The cold call is one of the most underrated sales tools there is. Most salespeople avoid it. Those who do pick up the phone often do it badly - and conclude that "cold calling doesn't work". It does. Just not the way they do it.

Over the years I have made and listened to thousands of these calls. Behind almost every failed call, there is usually one of these five mistakes.

## Mistake 1: You start with yourself, not the customer

The first sentence decides everything. "Good morning, I'm John from company X and I'd like to present our offer" is a recipe for being hung up on. Within three seconds the customer has filed you under "just another salesperson" and is already looking for a way to end the call.

Instead, start with the value for the customer: "I'm calling because I can see your company is expanding into new markets - we work with several companies in that situation and have helped them shorten their sales cycle." The difference is simple: the first sentence was about you, the second about them. That is enough to keep the customer on the line for a dozen or so seconds longer - and that is exactly what you need.

## Mistake 2: You talk too much

A cold call is not a presentation. Your goal is to book a conversation, not to sell over the phone. Yet as soon as the customer doesn't put the phone down, most salespeople start burying them in information - as if the amount you say had anything to do with how effective you are.

The rule is the opposite: talk less than you listen. Every sentence you deliver is a sentence the customer isn't saying about themselves - and it is from their words that you will build the reason for a meeting. Ask questions that open up the conversation, and let them be the one to fill it.

## Mistake 3: You are afraid of objections

"We have no budget", "We already have a supplier", "Send an email" - these are not refusals. They are reflexes. The customer says them automatically, before they have even processed why you are calling. A salesperson who treats them as the end of the conversation ends it themselves.

Every objection is an invitation to ask another question. "We already have a supplier" doesn't mean "I'm not talking to you" - it means "convince me it's worth giving you five minutes". The point is not to force the objection down, but to look behind it. Very often, what the customer says first is not the real reason.

## Mistake 4: You have no structure

Improvisation only works when you have thousands of conversations behind you - and even then, the best have a skeleton in their heads. At the beginning, you need a script. Not to read aloud word for word, because that can be heard and sounds lifeless - but as a map: where do I start, where am I heading, how do I react to the most common responses.

A salesperson without structure gets lost after the first unexpected answer from the customer. A salesperson with structure knows where they are in the conversation and what comes next. That confidence is audible on the other end - and it is what builds trust, not smooth formulas.

## Mistake 5: You don't analyse your results

How many calls did you make? How many of them turned into a conversation? How many into a booked meeting? If you don't know these numbers, you are not selling - you are just dialling and hoping.

Without data there is no improvement, because you don't know what to improve. Maybe the problem is the opening and customers hang up in the first few seconds. Maybe it is the close, and great conversations don't end with a date in the diary. Those are two completely different problems with two different solutions - and you will only tell them apart if you measure.

---

These are the five most common mistakes, but not the whole craft. Specific openings, objection handling and conversation structure are things we practise in training, on your real calls. Start with these five, though - simply eliminating them changes your results faster than you expect.
