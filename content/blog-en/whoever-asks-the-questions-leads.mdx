# Whoever Asks the Questions Leads the Conversation

There is a widespread belief that a good salesperson is one who talks a lot and talks well. That selling is a display of eloquence. It is one of the most expensive myths in this profession.

In reality, the person who controls the conversation is not the one who talks - it is **the one who asks the questions**. Because a question sets the direction. The person answering follows the person asking. Always.

## Talking gives away control

When a salesperson talks a lot, something happens that is not obvious at first glance: they hand over the initiative. The client sits, listens, evaluates - and waits for the moment to say "I need to think about it". The more you talk, the more material you give them for doubt.

When you ask a good question, the roles reverse. It is the client who does the work - thinking, explaining, realising things they had not thought about before. And the best part is that the arguments they arrive at themselves are ten times stronger for them than the ones you handed them.

## The best salesperson gets the client to convince themselves

This is the heart of it. The point is not to convince the client with your arguments. The point is to ask the kind of questions that let **the client convince themselves**.

People don't argue with their own conclusions. If I tell you that you are losing money on your current solution, you will defend yourself. If you get there yourself by answering my question, it is your thought, not mine. And suddenly I don't have to convince you of anything.

That is why in a meeting I keep to a simple proportion: I talk less than I listen. Not because I am quiet, but because every minute the client spends talking brings me closer to a sale than a minute I spend talking.

## It's a skill, not a talent

The good news: asking the right questions can be learned. It is not a matter of charisma or being a smooth talker. It is a technique - specific, repeatable, something you can practise.

I won't write out a list of the questions I use here - because a question pulled out of context is useless, and the whole art lies in what to ask and when. We work through that together, on your real calls. But remember the rule right now: **the next time you feel you are losing the conversation - stop talking and ask a question.** Control will come back to you in a single sentence.
